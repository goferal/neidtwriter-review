Work About Non-Billable Contact

I get paid to pay attention.

Creative director and brand strategist. Which is a formal way of saying I find the true thing, and then I write it down.

I started as a copywriter, and I still am one — but somewhere along the way the interesting problems stopped being about sentences and started being about what the sentences were supposed to mean.

Now I architect brand foundations, build B2B positioning strategies, and run multi-channel creative direction. I've done it inside agencies as a copywriter and associate creative director, and independently since 2013, working directly with brands and alongside shops like BBDO, Wolff Olins, Publicis and Fast Horse.

The through-line is the same one it always was. Somebody has to notice the thing everybody else stopped seeing, and then find language exact enough that a company of sixty thousand people can repeat it without wearing it out. That's the job. Writing is how I prove I've done it.

I'm also a former poet, a sometime teacher, an oxford comma partisan, and shorter than you'd imagine.

How I work

Four things I'd want you to know before we start.

01 — Notice first

The brief is never the problem. The problem is the thing nobody in the room has said out loud yet. I'll ask a lot of questions before I write anything.

02 — Say it plain

If it needs a glossary, it isn't finished. Clarity isn't the opposite of craft — it's the proof of it.

03 — Make the connection

Most good ideas are two known things nobody had bothered to put next to each other. That's not a trick. That's the whole discipline.

04 — Leave it better

Good work makes somebody feel a certain way. Good. Or confident. Or assured. If nobody feels anything, it was just output.

Working together

Mostly it's just me, which is the point — you get the person who did the thinking, not a team that inherited it. When a project needs more hands, I assemble them and stay accountable for the whole.

I work directly with brands, and I work as an extra brain inside agencies when a client's problem turns out to be a language problem. I've been on both sides enough to be useful in either.

I'm best with what I'd call craft clients — any size, any industry, as long as they're genuinely invested in the thing they make and willing to be honest about what isn't working yet.
