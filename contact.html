Before you ask

The questions I get every time.

Answers below are drafted in Jeff's voice as a starting point — he should rewrite them until they sound like him. Draft — TK

01 What if we don't know what we need yet?

Then you're in the right place, and honestly that's most of my work. "We know something's off but we can't name it" is a better starting point than a tidy brief, because a tidy brief usually means somebody already decided the answer and just wants it written up.

The first conversation costs nothing. If it turns out you need something I don't do, I'll tell you that and probably tell you who does.

02 What does a typical engagement look like?

There isn't really a typical one, which is part of why I like this. It ranges from a two-week positioning sprint to a brand foundation that runs for months and ends with a system your team can use without me.

What's consistent: I start by asking more questions than you expect, I show thinking before I show finished work, and I'd rather kill a direction early than defend it late. Jeff to make specific

03 Do you work through agencies?

Often. I've worked alongside BBDO, Wolff Olins, Publicis, Fast Horse, KNOCK and a few dozen others — sometimes as the strategist, sometimes as the writer who makes the strategy survive contact with a page.

I'm comfortable inside your process and I'm comfortable being invisible to your client. That's a normal part of the job.

04 Can you lead a project, or only write it?

Lead it. That's the shift this site is about. I've been the copywriter, the associate creative director, and the person responsible for whether the whole thing holds together — and these days it's usually the last one.

The writing is still how I prove the thinking is real. It just isn't the ceiling.

05 How do you charge?

Project rate wherever possible, because hourly billing punishes the part of the work that matters most — the thinking that happens before anything gets typed.

I'll give you a number after the first conversation, not before. Jeff to confirm how much to say here

06 What's the smallest thing worth calling you about?

A sentence. Genuinely. Some of the best engagements I've had started with someone asking me to fix one line on a homepage, and the line turned out to be a symptom of a positioning problem nobody had looked at in six years.

If it's actually just the one line, I'll fix it and we'll both get on with our day.

Why the FAQ lives here

An FAQ page in the nav is a page nobody navigates to. An FAQ under Contact catches people at the exact moment of hesitation — they've decided they're interested and are looking for a reason not to send the email. Question 01 is deliberately first; it's the highest-converting question on any consultant's site.
