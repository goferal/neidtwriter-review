3M · Foundations

Science Applied to Life

3M's first global brand campaign in more than twenty-five years — and the internal system that let sixty thousand products speak with one voice.

Hero image TK — campaign key visual
01 — The situation

3M makes something like sixty thousand products, sold into five business groups that had spent decades building their own languages. The company was enormously successful and almost entirely invisible — people knew Post-it and Scotch, not the company that made them.

The task was a single global brand platform, and — harder — a way for every division to use it without flattening what made them different.

02 — What I noticed

The average person touches 3M technology a hundred times a day and can't name the company once.

Which reframed the whole thing. It was never an awareness problem — people were surrounded by the work. It was an attribution problem. The platform didn't need to explain what 3M makes. It needed to connect the science people never see to the life they're already living.

03 — What we made

Brand platform copy carried across all five business groups. A brand training hub so teams inside 3M could apply the platform themselves. Rewritten business group pages. Supporting social and digital.

Then the follow-through, which is the part that actually mattered: a newsroom content engine that produced more than two hundred stories in ten months, proving the platform could carry real editorial weight rather than sitting in a guidelines PDF.

04 — What happened

The campaign launched in March 2015 as 3M's first global brand campaign in more than twenty-five years, developed with Wolff Olins on identity and strategy and BBDO New York and BBDO Minneapolis on campaign. Forbes covered the launch.

No performance figures are public, and I'm not going to invent any. What I'd point at instead is longevity: the platform outlasted the launch by years, which is the only real test of whether a brand foundation was built properly.
